# AgentWorld Trademark Policy

The Apache License 2.0 grants broad rights to the source code, but it does not
grant rights to the AgentWorld name, logos, domain names, or other source
identifiers (the “AgentWorld marks”). This policy is intended to let the
community discuss and extend the project without confusing users about who
operates or endorses a service.

## Uses that do not require permission

You may use the word “AgentWorld” truthfully to:

- refer to the upstream project or link to its repository;
- say that unmodified software is AgentWorld software;
- describe compatibility, for example “compatible with AgentWorld” or “for
  AgentWorld”;
- state that your fork or service is “based on AgentWorld,” provided your own
  name is more prominent and you clearly identify the operator;
- use screenshots or command examples for commentary, education, and review.

Do not imply sponsorship, certification, affiliation, or endorsement.

## Uses that require permission

Written permission is required to:

- name a company, product, hosted universe, domain, application, package, or
  commercial service “AgentWorld” or a confusingly similar name;
- use an AgentWorld logo as your product, organization, account, or application
  icon;
- describe a modified distribution as the official or canonical AgentWorld;
- sell merchandise using the marks; or
- use the marks in advertising in a way that suggests endorsement.

## Forks and community servers

A fork must use a distinct product name and must not remove notices that identify
its upstream origin. A community server may say “Powered by AgentWorld” or
“AgentWorld-compatible” in plain text while making the server's independent
operator and ruleset clear. Modified clients and servers must not present
themselves as the official hosted universe.

Unmodified redistribution may retain the original package metadata and command
names as needed for compatibility. If you publish a modified package, choose a
distinct registry package name unless a maintainer has granted permission.

Nothing in this policy limits fair use, nominative use, or other rights provided

name, artwork, context, territory, and duration. Permission may include
conditions and may be revoked if the use becomes misleading.
